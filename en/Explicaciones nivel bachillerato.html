High-school physics guide

Everything that usually comes up in high-school physics courses and entrance exams: key ideas, formulas, a step-by-step worked problem and exercises that are generated at random and grade themselves. At the end there's the complete formula sheet to print.

How to use this guide

1) Read the key ideas. 2) Try the example before opening the steps. 3) Solve at least three exercises in a row without mistakes before moving on to the next topic. Use g = 9.81 m/s² unless stated otherwise and round to two decimals.

1. Quantities, units and scientific notation

  • The International System (SI) uses the meter (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), ampere (A), kelvin (K), mole and candela.
  • To convert, multiply by factors equal to 1: \(72\ \tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}\cdot\tfrac{1000\ \text{m}}{1\ \text{km}}\cdot\tfrac{1\ \text{h}}{3600\ \text{s}}=20\ \text{m/s}\). Shortcut: from km/h to m/s, divide by 3.6.
  • Scientific notation: \(0.00045=4.5\times10^{-4}\). Prefixes: kilo (10³), mega (10⁶), giga (10⁹), milli (10⁻³), micro (10⁻⁶), nano (10⁻⁹).
  • Significant figures: the result can't be more precise than the least precise piece of data.
Common mistake

Mixing units: using speed in km/h with time in seconds. Convert everything to SI before substituting.

2. Vectors

  • A scalar has only magnitude (mass, time, temperature); a vector has magnitude and direction (velocity, force, displacement).
  • Components: \(A_x=A\cos\theta\), \(A_y=A\sin\theta\). Magnitude: \(A=\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2}\). Direction: \(\theta=\tan^{-1}(A_y/A_x)\) (watch out for the quadrant).
  • To add vectors, add the components separately.
Example: a boat sails 30 km east and then 40 km north. What is its displacement?
  1. Components: \(R_x=30\ \text{km}\), \(R_y=40\ \text{km}\).
  2. Magnitude: \(R=\sqrt{30^2+40^2}=\sqrt{2500}=50\ \text{km}\).
  3. Direction: \(\theta=\tan^{-1}(40/30)=53.1^\circ\) north of east.
  4. Note: the distance traveled was 70 km, but the displacement is 50 km.

3. Uniform motion, uniformly accelerated motion and free fall

  • UM (constant velocity): \(d=vt\).
  • UAM (constant acceleration): \(v=v_0+at\), \(d=v_0t+\tfrac12at^2\), \(v^2=v_0^2+2ad\), \(d=\tfrac{v_0+v}{2}\,t\).
  • Free fall: it's UAM with \(a=g=9.81\ \text{m/s}^2\) downward. From rest: \(h=\tfrac12gt^2\), \(v=gt\), \(v=\sqrt{2gh}\).
  • In an upward vertical throw, at the highest point the velocity is zero, but the acceleration is still g.
Example: a stone is dropped from a bridge and takes 2.5 s to reach the water. How high is the bridge and how fast does it hit?
  1. Given: \(v_0=0\), \(t=2.5\ \text{s}\), \(g=9.81\ \text{m/s}^2\).
  2. \(h=\tfrac12gt^2=\tfrac12(9.81)(2.5)^2=30.66\ \text{m}\).
  3. \(v=gt=(9.81)(2.5)=24.53\ \text{m/s}\) (about 88 km/h).

4. Projectile motion

  • It splits into two independent motions: horizontal (UM, \(v_x=v_0\cos\theta\)) and vertical (UAM, \(v_{0y}=v_0\sin\theta\), \(a=-g\)).
  • Flight time (same level): \(t_v=\dfrac{2v_0\sin\theta}{g}\). Maximum height: \(H=\dfrac{v_0^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}\). Range: \(R=\dfrac{v_0^2\sin2\theta}{g}\).
  • Maximum range is reached at 45°, and complementary angles (30° and 60°) give the same range.

5. Newton's laws and friction

  • Always draw the free-body diagram and choose axes. Apply \(\sum F_x=ma_x\) and \(\sum F_y=ma_y\).
  • Weight \(W=mg\). On a horizontal surface with no other vertical forces, \(N=mg\).
  • Friction: \(f_s\le\mu_sN\) (before moving), \(f_k=\mu_kN\) (while moving).
  • Inclined plane: \(N=mg\cos\theta\), the component of weight along the plane \(mg\sin\theta\).
Example: you push a 20 kg box with 100 N across a floor with μₖ = 0.3. What is its acceleration?
  1. \(N=mg=(20)(9.81)=196.2\ \text{N}\).
  2. \(f_k=\mu_kN=(0.3)(196.2)=58.86\ \text{N}\).
  3. \(F_{neta}=100-58.86=41.14\ \text{N}\).
  4. \(a=F_{neta}/m=41.14/20=2.06\ \text{m/s}^2\).

6. Work, energy and power

  • Work: \(W=Fd\cos\theta\). Kinetic energy: \(E_k=\tfrac12mv^2\). Potential: \(E_p=mgh\). Elastic: \(E_e=\tfrac12kx^2\).
  • Without friction, \(E_k+E_p\) is conserved. With friction, the difference is the work done by friction.
  • Power: \(P=W/t=Fv\). 1 hp ≈ 746 W.

7. Momentum and collisions

  • Momentum: \(\vec p=m\vec v\). Impulse: \(\vec J=\vec F\Delta t=\Delta\vec p\).
  • In every collision (isolated system) it is conserved: \(m_1v_1+m_2v_2=m_1v_1'+m_2v_2'\).
  • Perfectly inelastic collision (they stick together): \(v'=\dfrac{m_1v_1+m_2v_2}{m_1+m_2}\). Elastic collision: kinetic energy is also conserved.

8. Hydrostatics: pressure, Pascal and Archimedes

  • Density \(\rho=m/V\) (water: 1000 kg/m³). Pressure \(P=F/A\), in pascals (Pa).
  • Hydrostatic pressure: \(P=\rho gh\); absolute pressure \(P=P_{atm}+\rho gh\).
  • Pascal: a pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally throughout the fluid. Hydraulic press: \(F_1/A_1=F_2/A_2\).
  • Archimedes: buoyant force \(E=\rho_{fluid}\,g\,V_{submerged}\). A body floats if its average density is less than the fluid's.

9. Heat and temperature

  • Scales: \(T(\text{K})=T(^\circ\text{C})+273.15\); \(T(^\circ\text{F})=\tfrac95T(^\circ\text{C})+32\).
  • Sensible heat: \(Q=mc\Delta T\) (water: c = 4186 J/kg·°C = 1 cal/g·°C). Latent heat: \(Q=mL\).
  • Linear expansion: \(\Delta L=\alpha L_0\Delta T\).
  • Heat transfer: conduction, convection and radiation.

10. Electricity: Coulomb, Ohm and circuits

  • Coulomb's law: \(F=k\dfrac{|q_1q_2|}{r^2}\), \(k=8.99\times10^9\ \text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2\).
  • Ohm's law: \(V=IR\). Power: \(P=VI=I^2R=V^2/R\).
  • Series: \(R_{eq}=R_1+R_2+\dots\). Parallel: \(\dfrac1{R_{eq}}=\dfrac1{R_1}+\dfrac1{R_2}+\dots\)
  • Energy used: \(E=Pt\); on your bill, in kWh.

11. Waves and sound

  • \(v=\lambda f\), \(T=1/f\). Transverse waves (string, light) and longitudinal waves (sound).
  • Speed of sound in air ≈ 343 m/s at 20 °C. The human ear hears from 20 Hz to 20 kHz.
  • Doppler effect: an approaching source sounds higher; a receding one, lower.

12. Geometrical optics

  • Reflection: \(\theta_i=\theta_r\). Refractive index: \(n=c/v\). Snell: \(n_1\sin\theta_1=n_2\sin\theta_2\).
  • Lenses and mirrors: \(\dfrac1f=\dfrac1{d_o}+\dfrac1{d_i}\), magnification \(m=-\dfrac{d_i}{d_o}\). Real image if \(d_i>0\).
  • Lens power: \(P=1/f\) (diopters, with f in meters).
