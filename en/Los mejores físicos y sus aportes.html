Their stories

Also influenced physics

They weren't physicists, but their ideas crossed paths with physics in surprising ways.

Leonardo da Vinci

1452–1519 · Italy

In his notebooks he described that friction doesn't depend on contact area and is proportional to weight, two centuries before Amontons. He also studied the optics of the eye and the flight of birds.

Charles Darwin

1809–1882 · England

His theory of evolution needed a very old Earth. Lord Kelvin, using cooling calculations, gave it only tens of millions of years. When radioactivity (the Earth's internal heat) was discovered, it was shown that the Earth is about 4.5 billion years old.

Gregor Mendel

1822–1884 · Austrian Silesia

Before his famous peas, he studied physics at the University of Vienna with Christian Doppler, of the Doppler effect. His statistical analysis of heredity owes a lot to that training.

Louis Pasteur

1822–1895 · France

In 1848 he discovered that certain molecules exist in two «mirror» versions that rotate polarized light in opposite directions: the birth of molecular chirality, a key idea in optics and chemistry.

Thomas Alva Edison

1847–1931 · United States

A prolific inventor (phonograph, commercial light bulb, direct-current power grids). He observed that a hot filament emits electrons in a vacuum, the «Edison effect», the basis of the vacuum tubes of early electronics.
